Disability in Colorado

Disability in Colorado is not evenly distributed; it reflects differences in health, aging, economic conditions, and access to care across communities. This dashboard examines how disability varies across race, sex, and age, and how these dimensions intersect to shape unequal health and functional outcomes. Rather than a single statewide number, we present a layered view that highlights where disability is most concentrated and which populations are most affected.

Colorado Statewide Overview

Start with the statewide disability rate, then explore selected counties and population-group patterns.

Statewide disability patterns

Colorado's overall disability rate (11.45%) masks substantial variation across counties and populations. While some counties report very low disability prevalence, others experience rates that are more than double the state average. These differences reflect uneven health conditions, aging patterns, occupational risks, and access to healthcare and supportive services.

Race and disability

Viewing disability through race reveals meaningful disparities across groups. In Colorado, disability rates are highest among American Indian/Alaska Native and Native Hawaiian/Pacific Islander populations, and lower among Asian populations. These differences reflect variations in health status, access to care, environmental exposures, and structural inequalities across racial groups.

Closing narrative

Disability reflects health, age, and access

Disability in Colorado reflects a combination of health, demographic, and structural factors rather than a single statewide condition. Addressing these disparities requires attention to aging populations, equitable access to healthcare and support services, and targeted strategies for communities experiencing the highest disability burden.
