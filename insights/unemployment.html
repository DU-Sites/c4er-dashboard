Unemployment in Colorado

Unemployment in Colorado is not evenly distributed; it reflects differences in access to stable work, local economic conditions, and structural inequalities across communities. This dashboard examines how unemployment varies across race, sex, and age, and how these dimensions intersect to shape labor market vulnerability. Rather than a single statewide number, we present a layered view that highlights where joblessness is most concentrated and which populations are most affected.

Statewide unemployment patterns

Colorado's overall unemployment rate (4.63%) masks meaningful variation across counties and populations. While some counties experience near full employment, others face unemployment rates that are more than twice the state average. These differences reflect uneven labor market opportunities, industry composition, and local economic conditions that shape access to stable employment.

Closing narrative

Employment barriers are unevenly distributed

Unemployment in Colorado is shaped by intersecting social and economic inequalities rather than a single statewide condition. Addressing these disparities requires targeted workforce strategies, equitable access to employment opportunities, and policies that respond to the needs of populations facing the greatest barriers to stable work.
