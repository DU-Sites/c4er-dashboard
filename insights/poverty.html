Poverty in Colorado

Poverty in Colorado is not evenly distributed; it reflects deep structural differences across communities, places, and social positions. This dashboard examines how poverty varies across race, sex, and age, and how these dimensions intersect to shape unequal risk. Rather than a single statewide number, we present a layered view that highlights where disparities are most concentrated and which populations are most affected.

Colorado Statewide Overview

Start with the statewide poverty rate, then explore selected counties and population-group patterns.

Statewide poverty patterns

Colorado's overall poverty rate (9.44%) masks substantial variation across counties and populations. While some counties experience relatively low poverty levels, others face rates that are two to three times higher. These differences are not random; they reflect uneven economic opportunity, demographic composition, and local conditions that shape access to resources and stability.

Race and poverty

Viewing poverty through race reveals persistent and substantial disparities across groups. In Colorado, poverty is systematically higher among American Indian/Alaska Native and Black populations, while lower among White and some Asian populations. These differences reflect historical and structural inequalities that shape access to income, employment, and opportunity across racial groups.

Poverty is shaped by place and population

Poverty in Colorado is not a uniform condition; it is shaped by intersecting social and geographic inequalities. Addressing these disparities requires moving beyond statewide averages toward targeted, equity-focused strategies that respond to the specific needs of high-risk populations and communities.
